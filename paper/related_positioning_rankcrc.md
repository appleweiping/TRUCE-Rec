# Related Work & Positioning — RankCRC

> Current positioning for the RankCRC headline (replaces the stale CU-GR `related_positioning.md`, kept for history). Locked / experiment-independent. The contribution is **distribution-free risk-controlled selective reliability** for recommendation, not raw-ranking SOTA.

## Conformal prediction and conformal risk control
Split conformal prediction (Vovk; Lei; Angelopoulos & Bates, *A Gentle Introduction to Conformal Prediction*) gives distribution-free coverage for set-valued predictors; Conformal Risk Control (Angelopoulos, Bates, et al., 2024) extends this to any bounded, monotone loss, yielding `E[L(λ̂)] ≤ α` in marginal expectation. RCPS / Learn-then-Test (Bates et al. 2021; Angelopoulos et al. 2021) give the high-probability (PAC) analogue via multiple-testing. **RankCRC's difference:** the controlled object is **listwise rank-risk** (`1 − NDCG@k`) on the **served slice after abstention** (a self-normalized conditional mean), not a pointwise loss over the full population. We show plain CRC does not attach to the naive served-slice estimator (random-denominator winner's curse) and repair it with a frozen-`λ` B1/B2 split so the served mean is an honest i.i.d. mean; we name LTT as the PAC upgrade and a numerator+coverage-floor route as an exact surrogate. To our knowledge this is the first distribution-free *served-slice rank-risk* certificate for recommendation, driven by a *label-free* selector.

## Selective prediction / classification with a reject option
SelectiveNet (Geifman & El-Yaniv) and the risk–coverage literature learn an abstention head trading coverage for accuracy, but provide **no distribution-free finite-sample risk guarantee** — the risk–coverage curve is empirical. **RankCRC's difference:** it adds the CRC marginal-expectation certificate on top of abstention, and the selector is *label-free* over rank-geometry features rather than a jointly-trained confidence head. We include a SelectiveNet-without-guarantee baseline (trained on Fold A only, matched coverage) precisely to isolate that the *guarantee* — not merely "abstain on hard users" — is the contribution.

## LLM-uncertainty calibration
Verbalized-confidence and calibration work (ECE, temperature scaling, reliability diagrams; Kadavath et al.; Tian et al.) asks whether an LLM's stated confidence matches accuracy. **RankCRC makes no ECE / reliability-diagram claim**: the object is an operational *served-slice risk certificate*, a different and stronger deployment guarantee than calibration of a confidence number. Our confidence `g` is not the LLM's verbalized confidence but a logistic model over rank-geometry of the base ranker's candidate scores, with the held-out label forbidden as a feature.

## Selective / reliable recommendation and LLM-for-RecSys
Prior LLM-for-recommendation work (the 8 official baselines: ELMRec, IRLLRec, LLM2Rec, LLMEmb, LLM-ESR, ProEx, ProMax, RLMRec) targets raw-ranking improvement on the full user population. Selective / cautious recommendation has been explored heuristically (confidence thresholds, popularity gating) without distribution-free guarantees. **RankCRC's difference:** it reframes the problem as *certified reliability* — serve only when `E[1−NDCG@k | served] ≤ α` provably holds — orthogonal to raw SOTA, and demonstrates (the empirical contribution, GPU-gated) that a deployable label-free selector holds the bound across 8 domains with non-trivial coverage and transfers across domains/rankers. The sibling pointwise-posterior method (pony) owns the raw-ranking lane; TRUCE owns the reliability lane.

## Why this is not "CRC applied to NDCG"
The strongest reviewer objection is incrementality. The novelty is the *combination*: (i) listwise rank-risk as the controlled quantity; (ii) the served-slice selective framing; (iii) a **label-free, deployable** confidence over rank-geometry (no test peek), repaired with the frozen-`λ` split so the conditional-mean certificate is valid; (iv) a *falsifiable* gain law that predicts the served-slice lift a priori; and (v) the 8-domain feasibility triad (cross-domain risk control + non-trivial coverage at useful `α` + label-free confidence transfer) — which no prior conformal-recsys work demonstrates. The value is orthogonal to raw SOTA: a certified "serve only when the rank-risk is bounded" is operationally useful even when raw ranking is mid-pack.
