Large language models (LLMs) are now routinely used to score or re-rank items in recommendation pipelines. Most of this work pursues a single objective: a *better ranking* over a candidate set. Yet a recommender that is excellent on average can still be unreliable for *particular* users — the held-out next item falls outside the model's confident region, and the served ranking is no better than a random reorder. In deployment this matters more than a fractional NDCG gain: serving a low-quality ranking to a user is a concrete cost, and a system that could instead *decline to intervene* (defer to a safe fallback, withhold a push, or ask for more signal) would avoid that cost — **if** it knew, per user and with a guarantee, when its ranking is trustworthy. The question we study is therefore not "how do we rank better?" but **"for which users can we certify that the ranking we serve meets a target quality, using only signals available at inference?"**

This is a *selective* recommendation problem, and three existing toolkits each fall short of it. **(i) LLM rerankers** (pointwise, pairwise, or listwise) improve the ranking but say nothing about when to trust it. **(ii) Heuristic cautious recommendation** — confidence thresholds, popularity gating — can abstain, but offers no distribution-free guarantee on the risk of what it serves; its risk–coverage curve is empirical and may not hold out of sample. **(iii) LLM-uncertainty calibration** (verbalized confidence, ECE, reliability diagrams) calibrates a *number*, not an *operational risk bound* on a downstream ranking metric. None delivers a finite-sample, distribution-free statement of the form "on the users we serve, the expected rank-risk is at most α," driven by a selector that reads no test label.

We propose **RankCRC** (Risk-Controlled Selective Recommendation). Given any base ranker and a per-user **label-free-at-inference** confidence `g` — a logistic model over 24 rank-geometry features (score margins, softmax peakiness, responsibility-entropy order statistics, MC-dropout variance, popularity), with the held-out relevance forbidden as a feature — RankCRC abstains on low-confidence users and controls the *served-slice listwise rank-risk* `1 − NDCG@k`. Concretely, it freezes `g` on one fold and the abstention threshold `λ` on a second fold, then certifies on a disjoint third fold, so that for the served sub-population the **Conformal-Risk-Control marginal-expectation bound** holds: `E[ 1 − NDCG@k | served ] ≤ E_{B2}[α]`, conditional on the frozen selector. The guarantee is honest about its class — marginal in expectation over the calibration/test draw, **not** PAC/high-probability, not per-user, not a fixed-served-size bound — and the frozen-`λ` split is what makes the conditional-mean control valid rather than a post-hoc fit (a subtlety we make precise, and adversarially verify, in the method). Because the metric is fixed-`k` NDCG@k over a frozen scorer, the abstention threshold is the *only* risk dial (`λ ≡ τ`); a distinct knob would require a changed objective.

RankCRC is deliberately a **reliability** contribution, orthogonal to raw-ranking SOTA, and we state the trade-off openly: on Amazon Beauty the base raw NDCG@10 (0.1143) is *below* the strongest baseline (ProEx, 0.1506). RankCRC does not claim to out-rank; it adds a *guarantee on the served slice* and a deployable rule for *when to serve*. We do not claim a new conformal theorem either: once the selector is frozen, the served-slice control is standard risk estimation on the accepted sub-population. The contribution is a *protocol + application* — the recommendation-specific listwise served-slice formulation, the leakage-safe label-free selector with the frozen-`λ` discipline, a falsifiable served-slice gain law, and the cross-domain feasibility study.

**Preliminary evidence (CONTROLLED, CPU; full study [PENDING 8-domain GPU rollout]).** On Beauty, with the corrected B1/B2 procedure and the label-free selector, the marginal-expectation guarantee holds at every target coverage across 200 random resplits (mean served-risk ≤ the mean certificate at all five coverage levels), the risk–coverage Pareto is monotone (17/17 steps), and the selector separates high- from low-risk users with test AUC 0.627 versus 0.519 for a raw score-margin baseline. The full claim — that this holds *across 8 domains* with non-trivial coverage at deployment-relevant α, and that the selector transfers across domains and rankers — is the pre-registered feasibility deliverable of the 8-domain rollout **[PENDING]**; domains where the empirical mean exceeds α, or where useful α forces near-zero coverage, will be reported honestly with the realized gap.

Our contributions are:

1. **Served-slice selective-risk formulation for recommendation.** We define and control the *conditional* served-slice listwise rank-risk `E[1−NDCG@k | served]` via a frozen-`λ` three-fold (A/B1/B2/C) conformal-risk-control protocol, with a precise — and adversarially verified — marginal-expectation guarantee that does not overclaim (no PAC, per-user, or realized-certificate reading), plus a leakage-safe audit that we show has teeth (a negative-control test in which injecting a label-derived feature must fail the audit).
2. **A label-free-at-inference selector over rank-geometry.** A 24-feature logistic confidence that reads no test-fold label at deployment, making the abstention rule fully deployable; we benchmark it against a non-guaranteed SelectiveNet-style abstainer (trained on Fold A, matched coverage) and an oracle label-aware upper bound to isolate that the *guarantee*, not merely "abstain on hard users," is the contribution.
3. **A falsifiable selective-gain law.** A closed-form prediction of the served-slice lift, `ΔNDCG@k ≈ (2·AUC − 1)·Gini·κ(c)`, computed a priori from held-out quantities and pre-registered with an out-of-sample R² falsification test (demoted to a heuristic if it fails), kept as a secondary claim.
4. **Cross-domain feasibility [PENDING 8-domain GPU rollout].** Full-scale evidence (8 domains, 10k users / Beauty 973, 101 candidates, 8 frozen official baselines, paired Holm–bootstrap) that the guarantee holds, that useful operating points exist, and that the label-free selector transfers — with all failures reported honestly.
